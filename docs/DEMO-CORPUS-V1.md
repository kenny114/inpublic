## DC-01 — The Hard Part Wasn't Coding

**DOMAIN**  
Creator / building in public

**EXPECTED EXPRESSIVE FORM**  
Clean settled text with a reflective progression. This is an intentional restraint case.

**EXPECTED VISUAL FAMILY**  
NONE

**WHY THIS IS INTERESTING**  
It tests whether InPublic can let a personal thought breathe without manufacturing a diagram.

**SCRIPT**  
Building something is strange because I used to think the hard part was just coding it. Like, once the product worked, people would somehow understand it and everything after that would be easy. But now I feel like the real work starts when you have to explain why it matters, show it to people, hear what they actually think, and then keep going when the response is quiet. The coding is still hard, obviously, but at least code gives you an error. People don't always give you an error. Sometimes they just leave. So right now I'm learning that building the product and learning how to communicate the product are basically two different jobs.

**WHAT INPUBLIC SHOULD IDEALLY COMMUNICATE**  
A readable reflection that moves from the old belief, through the realization, to the final contrast between building and communicating.

**WHAT WOULD COUNT AS A BAD RESULT**  
A forced comparison graphic, invented categories, too many fragments, or camera motion that distracts from the thought.

**POTENTIAL PUBLIC CONTENT ANGLE**  
A candid building-in-public post about discovering that distribution and communication are part of the product job.

## DC-02 — Why Some Conversations Actually Help

**DOMAIN**  
Everyday communication

**EXPECTED EXPRESSIVE FORM**  
Text-only explanation with two contrasting kinds of listening, expressed through prose rather than a visual.

**EXPECTED VISUAL FAMILY**  
NONE

**WHY THIS IS INTERESTING**  
It is the second deliberate silence case and checks whether ordinary nuance remains clean and legible.

**SCRIPT**  
I think some conversations help you even when the other person doesn't give you advice. The difference is whether they are actually listening or just waiting for their turn to speak. When somebody is listening properly, you start hearing your own idea more clearly because you have enough space to finish it. You might even realize halfway through that the thing you were worried about is not really the main problem. But when somebody jumps in too early, now you are defending a version of the idea you hadn't even finished explaining. So sometimes the useful part of a conversation is not the answer. It is just having enough room to reach the real question.

**WHAT INPUBLIC SHOULD IDEALLY COMMUNICATE**  
The distinction between advice and useful listening, ending on the idea of reaching the real question.

**WHAT WOULD COUNT AS A BAD RESULT**  
An unnecessary two-column comparison, aggressive page turning, or a made-up listening process.

**POTENTIAL PUBLIC CONTENT ANGLE**  
A short thoughtful clip about why being heard can be more useful than being advised.

## DC-03 — What Actually Grows a Creator Account

**DOMAIN**  
Creator growth

**EXPECTED EXPRESSIVE FORM**  
Natural enumeration that emerges from one explanation rather than a numbered checklist.

**EXPECTED VISUAL FAMILY**  
Enumeration

**WHY THIS IS INTERESTING**  
Three related parts are clear enough to visualize, but the wording remains conversational.

**SCRIPT**  
The more I try to grow an account, the more it feels like three things have to work together. You need to put something out so people have a reason to notice you. You need to actually interact, because posting into an empty room and disappearing is not really building anything. And then you need some consistency, not in the fake sense where you post ten times every day, but enough that people can remember what you are about. I used to treat those as separate tricks. Now I think the real growth happens when the content, the conversations, and the consistency all support each other.

**WHAT INPUBLIC SHOULD IDEALLY COMMUNICATE**  
Three parts—content, interaction, and consistency—belonging to one creator-growth system.

**WHAT WOULD COUNT AS A BAD RESULT**  
Missing one of the three parts, duplicating them, inventing metrics, or drawing an unexplained sequence instead of a set.

**POTENTIAL PUBLIC CONTENT ANGLE**  
“The three things I am learning while trying to grow in public.”

## DC-04 — From Fifty Users to Five Hundred

**DOMAIN**  
Startup growth

**EXPECTED EXPRESSIVE FORM**  
Quantitative change followed by a qualitative interpretation.

**EXPECTED VISUAL FAMILY**  
Quantitative change

**WHY THIS IS INTERESTING**  
The numbers are meaningful to the story, while the conclusion warns against treating growth as the whole picture.

**SCRIPT**  
Imagine a small product grows from around fifty active users to five hundred in a couple of months. Obviously that looks exciting because the number is ten times bigger. But the interesting part is what happens underneath it. Maybe at fifty users the founder knew almost everybody and could answer every message personally. At five hundred, support starts taking real time, different people want different things, and one confusing part of the product gets repeated hundreds of times. So the growth is good, but it changes the job. You are not only trying to get more users anymore. You are trying to make sure the product can handle the attention it asked for.

**WHAT INPUBLIC SHOULD IDEALLY COMMUNICATE**  
The jump from 50 to 500 and the resulting shift from direct attention to scalable product/support needs.

**WHAT WOULD COUNT AS A BAD RESULT**  
Wrong arithmetic, a chart that implies continuous measured data, or visuals that bury the operational point.

**POTENTIAL PUBLIC CONTENT ANGLE**  
A founder lesson: growth does not just increase the number; it changes the job.

## DC-05 — Why Traffic Jams Appear From Nothing

**DOMAIN**  
Education / everyday science

**EXPECTED EXPRESSIVE FORM**  
Cause/effect explanation with a short chain of events.

**EXPECTED VISUAL FAMILY**  
Cause/effect

**WHY THIS IS INTERESTING**  
It has a strong, intuitive visual moment without requiring precise scientific claims or specialist vocabulary.

**SCRIPT**  
A traffic jam can start even when there is no accident and no roadblock. One driver taps the brakes because the car ahead gets a little close. The next driver reacts a bit later and brakes slightly harder. That keeps passing backward through the lane until cars way behind are almost stopping, even though the first car is already moving normally again. That is why you can sit in traffic for ten minutes, reach the front, and see absolutely nothing there. The jam was not one object blocking the road. It was a small slowdown getting amplified as every driver reacted to the person in front.

**WHAT INPUBLIC SHOULD IDEALLY COMMUNICATE**  
A small brake event propagating backward and becoming a larger slowdown.

**WHAT WOULD COUNT AS A BAD RESULT**  
Inventing an accident, reversing the causal direction, or producing a cluttered diagram that is harder than the explanation.

**POTENTIAL PUBLIC CONTENT ANGLE**  
“Why traffic sometimes disappears without you ever seeing the cause.”

## DC-06 — How a Plant Turns Light Into Growth

**DOMAIN**  
Education / biology

**EXPECTED EXPRESSIVE FORM**  
A simple process explanation with conservative scientific scope.

**EXPECTED VISUAL FAMILY**  
Sequence

**WHY THIS IS INTERESTING**  
It gives the sequence system a non-business example while remaining understandable without technical precision.

**SCRIPT**  
The simple way I think about photosynthesis is that a plant is collecting ingredients from different places. The leaves take in light from the sun and carbon dioxide from the air. The roots bring up water from the soil. Inside the plant, those ingredients are used to make sugar, which is basically stored energy the plant can use to grow. Oxygen is released along the way. There is obviously more chemistry happening than that, but the useful picture is this: light, air, and water come in, the plant turns them into usable energy, and that energy helps build more of the plant.

**WHAT INPUBLIC SHOULD IDEALLY COMMUNICATE**  
Inputs of light, carbon dioxide, and water leading to stored energy/growth, with oxygen released.

**WHAT WOULD COUNT AS A BAD RESULT**  
Unsupported molecular detail, oxygen shown as an input, a rigid numbered list, or duplicated inputs.

**POTENTIAL PUBLIC CONTENT ANGLE**  
A clean “explain a familiar thing simply” product demonstration.

## DC-07 — Content Versus Direct Outreach

**DOMAIN**  
Business / marketing

**EXPECTED EXPRESSIVE FORM**  
Balanced comparison with different strengths and tradeoffs.

**EXPECTED VISUAL FAMILY**  
Comparison

**WHY THIS IS INTERESTING**  
The two sides are genuinely comparable without fake scoring or a forced winner.

**SCRIPT**  
If you are trying to find early customers, content and direct outreach do different jobs. Content can reach people you do not know, and one good post can keep working after you publish it, but it usually takes time before people trust you or even notice you. Direct outreach is almost the opposite. You can choose exactly who to speak to and learn from the response immediately, but it takes effort every single time and it can feel awkward if the message is generic. I don't think one replaces the other. Outreach gives you close conversations now, while content slowly builds a reason for more people to come to you later.

**WHAT INPUBLIC SHOULD IDEALLY COMMUNICATE**  
Content: broader, compounding, slower. Outreach: targeted, immediate learning, repeated effort. Neither is declared universally better.

**WHAT WOULD COUNT AS A BAD RESULT**  
Invented numerical scores, declaring a winner, mixing attributes between sides, or an unreadable comparison layout.

**POTENTIAL PUBLIC CONTENT ANGLE**  
An honest early-stage marketing comparison for founders.

## DC-08 — The Product Feedback Loop

**DOMAIN**  
Product / SaaS

**EXPECTED EXPRESSIVE FORM**  
A repeating process loop described through a realistic product example.

**EXPECTED VISUAL FAMILY**  
Sequence

**WHY THIS IS INTERESTING**  
It naturally moves through observation, interpretation, change, and renewed observation.

**SCRIPT**  
A useful product feedback loop is not just asking users what feature they want and then building the loudest answer. You watch where people get stuck, you talk to them so you understand what they were trying to do, and then you make the smallest change that could actually help. After that, you put it back in front of people and see whether the problem got better or just moved somewhere else. That last part matters because a solution that sounds good in a conversation can still be confusing in the product. So the loop is really observe, understand, change, and observe again until the experience starts matching what people need.

**WHAT INPUBLIC SHOULD IDEALLY COMMUNICATE**  
A feedback cycle where renewed observation closes the loop rather than a one-way feature pipeline.

**WHAT WOULD COUNT AS A BAD RESULT**  
A linear diagram that loses the return to observation, or text claiming every request should be built.

**POTENTIAL PUBLIC CONTENT ANGLE**  
“What listening to users actually means when you are building a product.”

## DC-09 — How a Small SaaS Business Makes Money

**DOMAIN**  
Business / simple economics

**EXPECTED EXPRESSIVE FORM**  
Mixed explanation: a small system with flow and a quantitative relationship, without needing a forced visual.

**EXPECTED VISUAL FAMILY**  
Mixed / conservative; a useful system or cause/effect visual is welcome, but NONE is acceptable if evidence is insufficient.

**WHY THIS IS INTERESTING**  
It tests restraint when several possible structures are present and only one should be committed, if any.

**SCRIPT**  
A simple SaaS business sounds easy because customers pay a subscription every month, but that money is not automatically profit. The customers create revenue. Then the business has costs like hosting, software, support, and whatever it spends to find new customers. What remains after those costs is the part the business can actually keep or reinvest. The tricky part is that growth can increase both sides. More customers bring in more revenue, but they can also create more support and infrastructure costs. So the business works when the value of the recurring customers grows faster than the cost of serving and acquiring them.

**WHAT INPUBLIC SHOULD IDEALLY COMMUNICATE**  
Customers lead to revenue; costs reduce what remains; growth affects both revenue and cost.

**WHAT WOULD COUNT AS A BAD RESULT**  
Treating revenue as profit, inventing dollar amounts, committing multiple competing visuals, or oversimplifying costs away.

**POTENTIAL PUBLIC CONTENT ANGLE**  
A beginner-friendly explanation of the difference between subscription revenue and profit.

## DC-10 — Planning a Community Tournament

**DOMAIN**  
Community / operations

**EXPECTED EXPRESSIVE FORM**  
A deliberate multi-page explanation with a topic transition from preparation to match-day operation.

**EXPECTED VISUAL FAMILY**  
Mixed / multi-page; enumeration or sequence may be justified.

**WHY THIS IS INTERESTING**  
It creates enough natural structure for page behavior and camera review without saying “new page” or using detector language.

**SCRIPT**  
If I was planning a small community tournament, I would sort out the basic structure before worrying about graphics or promotion. You need the number of teams, the format, the rules, and a schedule that gives matches enough time without the whole thing running for ten hours. You also need one clear place where players can see updates, because confusion gets worse when information is spread across five chats.

Then match day becomes a different problem. Somebody has to confirm teams are ready, report results, handle disputes, and keep the next match moving. The best plan is not the one with the fanciest bracket. It is the one where everybody knows what is happening and one late team does not freeze the entire event.

**WHAT INPUBLIC SHOULD IDEALLY COMMUNICATE**  
Preparation on one coherent page and match-day operations on a later page, with the relationship between planning and smooth execution preserved.

**WHAT WOULD COUNT AS A BAD RESULT**  
A mid-thought page turn, every operational detail on separate pages, content clipped during arrival, or an invented tournament format.

**POTENTIAL PUBLIC CONTENT ANGLE**  
A multi-page product demo built around a familiar community task.

## DC-11 — What a Roamer Actually Does in MLBB

**DOMAIN**  
MLBB / esports

**EXPECTED EXPRESSIVE FORM**  
Relationship-led explanation with conservative visual commitment.

**EXPECTED VISUAL FAMILY**  
Conservative mixed; sequence or cause/effect only if clearly grounded.

**WHY THIS IS INTERESTING**  
It tests a real domain with specific language while asking InPublic not to invent unsupported tactical relationships.

**SCRIPT**  
In MLBB, the roamer is not just the person who stands next to the gold laner and protects them for the whole game. A good roamer is reading where pressure is needed. Early on, they might help the jungler secure space, check a bush, or move toward a lane where a fight could start. Around Turtle, their job changes again because vision, positioning, and starting or stopping the fight can decide the objective before the damage dealers even commit. That is why roamers can look quiet on the scoreboard and still control the match. Their value is often in making the map safer for their team and uncomfortable for the other one.

**WHAT INPUBLIC SHOULD IDEALLY COMMUNICATE**  
The roamer shifts support across jungle, lanes, and objectives; impact is not captured only by kills or damage.

**WHAT WOULD COUNT AS A BAD RESULT**  
Invented hero names, a fake fixed rotation, incorrect relationships, or a busy diagram pretending uncertain tactics are rules.

**POTENTIAL PUBLIC CONTENT ANGLE**  
An esports-domain clip showing the product handling natural specialist speech conservatively.

## DC-12 — Why Rain Doesn't Start in the Clouds

**DOMAIN**  
Education / weather

**EXPECTED EXPRESSIVE FORM**  
Cause/effect explanation that starts before the visible event.

**EXPECTED VISUAL FAMILY**  
Cause/effect

**WHY THIS IS INTERESTING**  
The title creates curiosity, and the explanation has a strong visual chain grounded in everyday experience.

**SCRIPT**  
Rain looks like it starts in the clouds, but the process really begins with water at the surface. Heat from the sun causes some water from the ocean, rivers, and even wet ground to evaporate into the air. As that warm, moist air rises, it cools down, and the water starts condensing into tiny droplets. Those droplets collect in clouds. When enough of them combine and become too heavy to stay suspended, they fall as rain. So the cloud is where we finally see the water gathering, but the whole cycle depends on water leaving the ground first and being carried back up into the atmosphere.

**WHAT INPUBLIC SHOULD IDEALLY COMMUNICATE**  
Surface water and heat lead to evaporation, rising/cooling leads to condensation, and accumulated droplets lead to rain.

**WHAT WOULD COUNT AS A BAD RESULT**  
Showing clouds as the original source of water, reversing steps, adding unsupported weather detail, or making the sequence unreadably dense.

**POTENTIAL PUBLIC CONTENT ANGLE**  
“Rain starts before there is a cloud”—a compact educational hook.

## Recording workflow

1. Approve or revise the scripts before recording.
2. Record one voice note, naturally, with the browser tab visible throughout processing.
3. Run only that file through `/demo-studio`.
4. Accept only a capture-integrity PASS.
5. Retain source audio, validated WebM, `session.json`, `transcript.txt`, `metadata.json`, and `thumbnail.png`.
6. Watch the actual WebM from beginning to end.
7. Complete the review record below and update the index before starting the next run.

Do not queue recordings. Do not rerun merely to change containers. Validated WebM is the canonical capture.
